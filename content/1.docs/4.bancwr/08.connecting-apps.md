## How apps connect

Nostr apps use Bancwr as a remote signer over NIP-46. An app sends requests through the bunker's relays, and the bunker answers them.

No app can connect on its own. An administrator issues a connection token for each app. The token:

- names the team member the app is connected for,
- names the identity the app signs as,
- lists the event kinds the app may sign, and any it needs approval for,
- says whether the app may encrypt and decrypt messages,
- sets how long the connection lasts, and how fast it may sign,
- works for one connection only, and expires if it is not used in time.

Every connected app signs as the bunker's identity, not the member's own key. "For a member" records who the app belongs to. It is not a separate key.

An app can only sign the event kinds its token allows. There is no "everything" option.

NIP-46 must be on, and the bunker needs at least 1 relay. See [The connection string apps use](/docs/bancwr/urls#the-connection-string-apps-use).

## The Connections page

Open **Connections** in the sidebar. Administrators and signers can open it.

![The Connected apps page, with a connected app, the token form and unused tokens](/img/screenshots/bancwr/connections.png)

- An administrator sees every connected app, and whom each one is for, and issues tokens.
- A signer sees the apps connected for them, under **Apps connected for you**.

Viewers do not have this page. A viewer never holds an app connection.

## Issuing a connection token

Only administrators can issue tokens.

1. Open **Connections**.
2. Under **Issue a connection token**, choose **For**: the member this app is connected for. It defaults to you. Only admins and signers are offered.
3. If the bunker holds more than one identity, choose **Sign as**: the identity the app signs as. Only identities the member may sign as are offered. See [Signing identities](/docs/bancwr/signing-identities).
4. Enter a **Label** to recognise the app later, such as "Damus on my phone".
5. Under **May sign**, choose what the app may sign:
   - **Notes and reactions** (kinds 1 and 7)
   - **Notes, reactions and reposts** (kinds 1, 6 and 7)
   - **Profile only** (kind 0)
   - **Choose kinds…**, then enter kind numbers separated by commas, such as `1, 7, 30023`.
   - **Nothing: encryption only**, for an app that only encrypts and decrypts.
6. Optional: under **Needs approval**, tick the kinds an administrator must approve before the bunker signs them. See [Approval before signing](/docs/bancwr/approval-before-signing).
7. Optional: under **May also**, tick any encryption the app may use. See [Encrypting and decrypting messages](#encrypting-and-decrypting-messages).
8. Choose how long the token is **Valid for**: 1 hour, 24 hours or 7 days. This is how long it can be used to connect.
9. Choose how long the connection lasts once the app connects, under **Connection lasts**: **Until revoked**, the default, or 1, 7, 30 or 90 days. See [Time limits](#time-limits).
10. Choose a **Rate limit**. The default, 60 a minute (burst 20), suits almost every app. See [Rate limits](#rate-limits).
11. Click **Issue token**.

None of these can be changed on an existing token or connection. To change one, issue a new token.

### The connection string

Bancwr shows a `bunker://` connection string and a QR code.

1. Give the string to the app, or scan the QR code with it. Use **Copy** to copy the string.
2. Click **Done** when the app has it.

::warning
The connection string is shown once. Anyone with it can connect until it is used or expires. The bunker keeps only a hash of its secret, so it cannot show it again. If you lose it, revoke the token and issue a new one.
::

If NIP-46 is turned off on the bunker, or it has no relays, Bancwr says so instead of issuing a token.

### Unused tokens

Tokens that have not been used yet, and have not expired, are listed under **Unused tokens**. Each shows its label, whom it is for, what it may sign, its limits, and when it expires.

Click **Revoke** to cancel a token before an app uses it.

### Tokens that are used up by a refused connection

If an app presents a token but the bunker refuses the connection, the token is used up. This happens when the token is for another identity, when its member has been removed, made a viewer or may no longer use the identity, or when the app asks only for permissions the token does not allow. The token shows as used, with no connection, and the app is told to ask an administrator for a new one. Issue a new token.

## Encrypting and decrypting messages

Some apps need to encrypt and decrypt with the brand's key: direct messages (NIP-17), private lists, and some others. Under **May also**, each is a separate permission:

- **Encrypt messages (NIP-44)**
- **Read encrypted messages (NIP-44)**
- **Encrypt messages (NIP-04, older apps)**
- **Read encrypted messages (NIP-04, older apps)**

NIP-44 is the current standard. Only older apps use NIP-04. Without a permission, the app is refused, and nothing is encrypted, decrypted or recorded.

Before allowing an app to read encrypted messages:

- It can read every encrypted message sent to the brand's key, from anyone. There is no way to limit it to particular senders.
- Each decrypt is recorded with the member, the app and the sender's key, but not the message.
- A direct-message inbox decrypts twice for each message, so it adds audit events quickly. Set a retention policy if the log should not grow without limit. See [Retention](/docs/bancwr/health-and-logs#retention).
- Revoking the app's connection stops it at once.

No app can encrypt or decrypt with the key of another app that has connected to the bunker, or that has sent it any request. This protects what apps and the bunker send each other.

## Time limits

A contractor, a campaign or a bot often needs access for a set time. Choose it under **Connection lasts**.

- The time counts from when the app connects, not from when the token is issued.
- When the time runs out, the bunker refuses the app and marks the connection ended: **Its time limit ran out**.
- Each connection shows when it ends, or "no time limit".
- A time limit cannot be extended. To give more time, issue a new token, and the app connects again.
- You can still revoke a connection before its time runs out.

## Rate limits

Every connection is rate limited, so a runaway bot or a compromised app cannot sign or decrypt without limit. Choose under **Rate limit**:

- **Default: 60 a minute (burst 20)**
- **10 a minute (burst 5)**
- **30 a minute (burst 10)**
- **120 a minute (burst 40)**
- **600 a minute (burst 100)**

The burst is how many requests the app can make at once before the per-minute rate applies.

- Signing, encrypting and decrypting count. Connecting, `ping` and asking for the public key do not.
- Past its limit, the app is refused and told how many seconds to wait. Normal use never comes near the default.
- The dashboard's **Requests refused (rate limit)** counts refusals since the bunker started. The bunker's log has a warning, and the audit log an event, at most once a minute for each connection that keeps being refused. A connection that shows up there again and again is worth a look: revoke it if it should not be doing that.
- Apps connected before the bunker had rate limits are limited to the default.

## Connected apps

Each connected app shows:

- The name, address and picture the app gave about itself, with an **Unverified** badge.
- **App key**: the app's own public key.
- **May sign**: the event kinds it was granted, and **May also**: any encryption it may use.
- Its rate limit, and when it ends, or "no time limit".
- When it connected, and when it last signed.
- Whom it is for (administrators only).

An app that gave no name is shown by its key.

### Names are not verified

NIP-46 lets an app name itself. The name, address and picture are what the app says, and Bancwr does not check them. They are never used to decide anything. Recognise your apps by the label and the time they connected.

## Revoking an app

1. Open **Connections**.
2. Click **Revoke** on the app.
3. Click **Revoke** again to confirm, or **Cancel**.

Signers can revoke the apps connected for them. Administrators can revoke any app.

The app can no longer sign once revoked.

### When a connection ends

A connection ends when:

- the app logs out,
- someone revokes it,
- its time limit runs out,
- the same app connects again with a newer connection, which replaces it,
- the member it was made for is removed from the team, or made a viewer,
- the member loses their grant for the identity it signs as,
- the bunker is restored from a backup.

Ended connections are hidden. Turn on **Show ended** to see them, with why and by whom each one ended.

Connections are kept across restarts of the bunker.

## What the bunker answers over NIP-46

The bunker supports these NIP-46 requests: `connect`, `get_public_key`, `sign_event`, `ping`, `switch_relays` (which changes nothing), `logout`, `nip44_encrypt`, `nip44_decrypt`, `nip04_encrypt` and `nip04_decrypt`. Each encryption request needs its own permission on the token.
