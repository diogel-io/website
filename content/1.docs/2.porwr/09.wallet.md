---
title: Wallet Management
description: Wallet Management is where Porwr manages Nostr Wallet Connect (NWC) connections and user-initiated Lightning wallet actions.
---


This feature is based on NIP-47. It lets Porwr talk to an external Lightning wallet service through Nostr relays.

## Important safety summary

- Porwr does not become a full Lightning wallet.
- Porwr does not custody your funds.
- Porwr stores NWC connection secrets inside the encrypted vault.
- Each Porwr account has its own wallets.
- Websites can use your wallet only through WebLN, after you enable it for that site.
- A website pays only from the active wallet of the account it is connected as.
- Every payment, from the extension or from a website, requires explicit approval. Payment approvals are never remembered.

## What Wallet Management is for

Use Wallet Management to:

- connect a wallet, either with a few clicks or by pasting an NWC URI
- label wallet connections and choose the active one
- store connection secrets in the encrypted vault
- fetch wallet capabilities
- check wallet balance
- pay BOLT11 invoices after explicit approval
- review payment history

## What Nostr Wallet Connect is

Nostr Wallet Connect lets an app send wallet commands over Nostr events.

Porwr holds a connection secret for your wallet service and uses it to send encrypted wallet requests over the
wallet's relays.

The wallet service decides which commands are allowed.

## Connecting a wallet

Open **Wallet Management** and choose **Connect wallet**.

### Connect with Alby Hub or Coinos

For Alby Hub and Coinos you don't need to copy anything.

1. Choose **Connect Alby Hub** or **Connect Coinos**.
2. Approve Porwr in the wallet window that opens.
3. Porwr stores the connection in the encrypted vault.

The connection key is created inside Porwr and is never shown to websites.

Each Porwr account has its own wallets. The connection is added to the account selected in the dashboard, so select the right account before you connect. See [Wallets and accounts](#wallets-and-accounts).

If you run your own Alby Hub, turn on **Use my own Alby Hub** and enter its `https://` address first. See
[Self-hosted Alby Hub](#self-hosted-alby-hub-umbrel-start9-and-home-nodes).

If the wallet window doesn't open, allow pop-ups for Porwr and try again.

### Paste a connection string

For any other Nostr Wallet Connect wallet, such as LNbits with NWC support, use
**Paste a connection string instead**.

1. In your wallet service, find **Apps**, **Connections**, **Developer**, or **Nostr Wallet Connect**.
2. Create a new app connection and copy the full `nostr+walletconnect://...` URI.
3. In Porwr, enter an optional **Connection label**, such as `Alby`, `LNbits`, or `Home node`.
4. Paste the URI into **NWC URI**.
5. Choose **Import connection**.

Porwr validates the URI and stores the secret in the encrypted vault.

An NWC URI contains the wallet service public key, relay URLs, a client secret and, optionally, a Lightning
address (`lud16`). Treat the full URI as sensitive. Do not share it publicly.

## Self-hosted Alby Hub (Umbrel, Start9 and home nodes)

If you run Alby Hub yourself, turn on **Use my own Alby Hub** and enter your hub's address before clicking **Connect Alby Hub**.

One-click connect only works when your hub is served over `https://` with a certificate your browser trusts. Porwr opens your hub's approval page in a pop-up and trusts the approval it sends back because of the page's address. Over plain `http://`, anything on your local network could alter or imitate that page, so Porwr refuses it.

Most home-node setups serve Alby Hub over plain `http://` on the local network, for example:

```text
http://umbrel.local:59000
```

With an address like this, one-click connect cannot work:

- An `http://` address is refused with **Enter the https:// address of your Alby Hub**.
- Changing it to `https://umbrel.local:59000` gets past that check, but the hub is not listening for HTTPS on that port. The approval page fails to load, or your browser warns about the certificate, and Porwr reports that it did not hear back from Alby Hub.

### Connect a self-hosted hub with a connection string

This works with any self-hosted hub, whatever its address. Payments do not use your hub's web address. They travel over the Nostr relays in the connection string.

1. Open Alby Hub at its usual local address, for example `http://umbrel.local:59000`.
2. Go to **Apps** and add a new app connection. Name it `Porwr`.
3. Grant at least **pay invoice**, **get balance** and **get info**, and set a budget if you want one.
4. Copy the full `nostr+walletconnect://...` connection string Alby Hub shows.
5. In Porwr, open **Wallet Management** and expand **Paste a connection string instead**.
6. Paste the string, add a label such as `Umbrel Alby Hub`, and click **Import connection**.

Porwr checks the connection straight away. If the check fails, make sure your hub is running and can reach its relay.

### Use one-click connect with a self-hosted hub

One-click connect works if your hub is served over HTTPS with a trusted certificate. Examples include a reverse proxy with a real certificate, Tailscale HTTPS, or a tunnel service. Enter that `https://` address under **Use my own Alby Hub**.

A self-signed certificate is not enough unless your browser already trusts it.

## Wallets and accounts

Each Porwr account has its own wallet connections, its own active wallet and its own payment history.

Wallet Management shows the wallets of the active account. The caption names it, for example "Wallets for Main
Account. Each account has its own wallets." To see and manage another account's wallets, make that account
active in [Key Management](/docs/porwr/keys). A wallet you connect belongs to the account that was active at the
time, and to no other.

### Upgrading from 0.2.x

Before 0.3.0, every account shared one list of wallets. When you upgrade, Porwr gives those wallets, and their
payment history, to the account aliased **Main Account**. If no account has that alias, they go to your first
account. This happens once, the first time you use a wallet feature after the upgrade.

If a wallet should belong to another account, connect it again while that account is selected, then remove it
from the main account.

### Removing an account

Removing an account from Porwr also removes its wallet connections, the WebLN access you gave websites for those
wallets, and its payment history. Your other accounts' wallets are not affected.

As with [removing a connection](#removing-a-connection), this does not revoke the connections in your wallet
service. Revoke them there too.

## Active wallet

Each account has one active wallet. Porwr uses it for that account's payments, including WebLN payments
requested by websites connected as that account.

Other connections are listed under **Other connections**. Use **Make active** to switch.

WebLN access is given for a particular wallet connection. After you make another wallet active, websites that
had WebLN enabled must ask you to enable it again before they can request payments.

## Configured connections

After connecting, Porwr shows each connection with:

- local label
- wallet service public key preview
- client public key preview
- configured relays
- advertised capabilities after fetching info
- last balance check when available

Connection summaries intentionally do not show the client secret.

## Wallet info

Click **Info** to ask the wallet service for its metadata and capabilities.

Capabilities may include:

- `get_info`
- `get_balance`
- `pay_invoice`
- `make_invoice`
- `lookup_invoice`
- `list_transactions`

The exact list depends on the wallet service and the permissions granted to the NWC connection.

## Balance checks

Click **Balance** to send a NIP-47 `get_balance` request.

If successful, Porwr shows the balance in sats.

If it fails, check:

- wallet service is online
- relays are reachable
- NWC connection is still valid
- wallet permits `get_balance`
- browser network access is working

## Paying invoices

Porwr supports explicit user-approved `pay_invoice` requests.

Workflow:

1. Click **Info** for the wallet connection.
2. Confirm `pay_invoice` appears in capabilities.
3. Click **Pay invoice**.
4. Paste a BOLT11 invoice.
5. Review parsed amount and invoice preview.
6. Tick the acknowledgement checkbox.
7. Click **Approve and pay**.

Start with a tiny test invoice, such as 1-10 sats.

## Why Pay invoice may be disabled

The Pay invoice button is disabled unless the wallet advertises `pay_invoice`.

If it is disabled:

1. click **Info**
2. check whether `pay_invoice` appears
3. verify the wallet connection grants payment permission

## Payment approval dialog

The approval dialog is intentionally direct because Lightning payments are real money.

It shows:

- selected wallet connection
- BOLT11 invoice field
- parsed amount where possible
- shortened invoice preview
- explicit acknowledgement checkbox
- final **Approve and pay** action.

Porwr only sends the wallet payment command after final approval.

## Payment history

Payment history is stored inside the encrypted vault.

It records the latest 100 payment attempts for each account, including payments requested by websites through
WebLN. Wallet Management shows the history of the selected account.

Entries include:

- timestamp
- wallet connection label
- status: succeeded or failed
- parsed amount when available
- fees when returned
- short payment hash when returned
- invoice preview
- error message for failed attempts

Porwr does not show the payment preimage in the UI.

## Websites and WebLN

Websites can ask Porwr to make Lightning payments through WebLN (`window.webln`). Nothing is paid without your
approval, and a site cannot use your wallet at all until you enable WebLN for it.

### Enabling WebLN for a site

The first time a site wants to use your wallet, it calls `webln.enable()`. Porwr shows a **WebLN wallet access
request** that says: "Allow this site to use Porwr as a WebLN wallet provider. Payments will still require
separate approval."

Under **Remember this choice**, pick how long the site keeps access:

- **Just this once**: until you leave or reload the page
- **For the next 8 hours**
- **Always for this site**

Enabling WebLN pays nothing by itself. It lets the site ask for payments.

### Approving each payment

When the site wants to pay an invoice, it calls `webln.sendPayment(invoice)`. Porwr shows a **WebLN payment
request** with the invoice amount. Approve it only if the amount is what you expect.

A payment can only be approved once. The approval shows "This request can only be approved once." and is never
remembered, so every payment asks again, even on a site where WebLN is enabled **Always for this site**.

### Which wallet a site pays from

A site pays from the [active wallet](#active-wallet) of the account it is connected as. The Porwr sidebar shows
this as **Connected as** for the site in the current tab. It is not necessarily your active account.

A site connected as one account can never pay from another account's wallets. If that account has no wallet, the
payment fails with "No wallet is configured for this account."

### Moving a site to another account

If you move a site to another account, for example with **Use _account_ for this site** in the sidebar or
**Reject and use _account_** on a request, its standing permissions are removed. The site must ask you to enable
WebLN again, and then pays from the new account's active wallet.

The same applies after you use **Make active** on another wallet for the site's account.

### Supported methods

Porwr supports the WebLN methods `enable`, `getInfo`, and `sendPayment`. Other methods, such as `keysend` and
`makeInvoice`, are rejected with an `UnsupportedMethodError`.

## Zaps from Nostr clients

Porwr 0.3.0 lets you zap from Nostr web clients such as Snort, Coracle, and nostrudel. The client builds the
zap. Porwr signs it and pays it, and asks for your approval at each step.

A zap from a website goes like this:

1. **The client reads your relays.** It calls `window.nostr.getRelays()` to fill in the zap request.
2. **You approve the zap request.** The client asks Porwr to sign a NIP-57 zap request (kind `9734`). The
   approval shows the amount, the recipient's npub, the note or event being zapped, and any comment. Signing
   attaches your identity to the zap. It does not pay anything. You can approve it **Just this once** or
   **For the next 8 hours**, but not always.
3. **You enable WebLN for the site.** The first time, the site calls `window.webln.enable()` and Porwr asks
   whether to let it request payments from the active wallet of the account the site is connected as. You can
   allow it **Just this once**, **For the next 8 hours**, or **Always for this site**. Enabling pays nothing by
   itself. See [Websites and WebLN](#websites-and-webln).
4. **You approve the payment.** The site calls `window.webln.sendPayment(invoice)`. Porwr shows the invoice
   amount and asks you to approve it. A payment approval is never remembered, so every zap asks again.

![A zap request approval, showing the amount, recipient, zapped event and comment](/img/screenshots/porwr/zap-approval.png){width="360"}

Porwr then pays the invoice through that account's active wallet connection, and the recipient's wallet
publishes the zap receipt.

### What Porwr does not do

- Porwr does not start zaps itself. There is no "send zap" button in the extension.
- It does not look up Lightning addresses, fetch invoices, or keep a zap history. Those are the client's job.
- It supports only some WebLN methods. See [Supported methods](#supported-methods).

### Another WebLN extension

If another extension, such as Alby, already provides `window.webln`, Porwr leaves it in place. Porwr's own
WebLN is then available as `window.diogelWebln`.

### Breaking change for developers

Porwr 0.3.0 removes `window.nostr.diogel.getZapCapabilities` and `window.nostr.diogel.sendZap`. Sites that
used them should build the zap request themselves, sign it with `window.nostr.signEvent`, and pay the invoice
with `window.webln.sendPayment`.

Zap history saved by an earlier version is dropped the next time the vault is saved.

## Removing a connection

Use **Remove** to delete a wallet connection from the vault.

You should also revoke unused or exposed NWC connections from the wallet service itself. Removing the local copy from Porwr does not necessarily revoke the credential on the wallet provider side.

## Security guidance

- Treat NWC URIs like API keys.
- Do not post NWC URIs in support tickets, screenshots, chats, or logs.
- Revoke exposed wallet connections immediately.
- Use low limits where your wallet service supports them.
- Start with small test payments.
- Do not approve invoices you do not understand.
- Enable WebLN only for sites you trust, and choose **Always for this site** only for sites you fully trust.
- On every payment approval, check the amount and the account the site is connected as.
- Remove old wallet connections you no longer use.

## Troubleshooting

### "Enter the https:// address of your Alby Hub"

You entered an `http://` address under **Use my own Alby Hub**. One-click connect needs an `https://` address with a trusted certificate. For a hub on Umbrel or another home node, connect it with a connection string instead. See [Self-hosted Alby Hub](#self-hosted-alby-hub-umbrel-start9-and-home-nodes).

### "We didn't hear back from Alby Hub"

The approval page did not report back to Porwr. Common causes:

- the window was closed before you approved Porwr
- the hub's address does not serve HTTPS, so the approval page never loaded
- the browser stopped at a certificate warning.

If you approved Porwr in your wallet, try again. For a self-hosted hub, use a connection string instead.

### Import fails

Check that the URI is complete and starts with `nostr+walletconnect://`.

It must include at least one relay and a valid secret.

### Info times out

Wallet service or relays may be offline. Try again later or check the wallet service.

### Balance times out

Check relay connectivity, wallet service status, and whether the NWC connection is still valid.

### Payment fails

Possible causes:

- invoice expired
- wallet has insufficient balance
- wallet refuses payment
- NWC connection lacks `pay_invoice` permission
- relays are unreachable
- wallet service is offline.

Failed attempts should appear in payment history with an error where available.

### A website says "No wallet is configured for this account."

The account the site is connected as has no active wallet. Check **Connected as** in the sidebar for that site.
Then either make that account active in Key Management and connect a wallet in Wallet Management, or move the
site to an account that has a wallet. See [Which wallet a site pays from](#which-wallet-a-site-pays-from).

### A website says "WebLN access has not been enabled for this site."

The site has not been enabled for the wallet it would pay from. This happens when:

- the access you chose has run out, for example after **Just this once** or **For the next 8 hours**
- the site was moved to another account
- you used **Make active** on another wallet for the site's account.

Let the site ask again, and approve the WebLN wallet access request.

### A website says "Vault is locked. Unlock Porwr before using WebLN."

Unlock Porwr and try the payment again.

### A website says "The active wallet connection does not advertise pay_invoice support."

The active wallet of the site's account cannot pay invoices. In Wallet Management, click **Info** to check its
capabilities, grant payment permission in your wallet service, or make a wallet that supports `pay_invoice`
active.

### A website payment is rejected

"WebLN payment was rejected by the user." means the payment request was rejected in Porwr. "WebLN access was
rejected by the user." means the WebLN wallet access request was rejected. If you meant to approve it, let the
site ask again.

### A website says the wallet did not answer in time

The payment may still complete. Check your wallet, or Payment history in Wallet Management, before you try again,
so you don't pay twice.

### A website was moved to another account

A site moved to another account loses its WebLN access and pays from the new account's active wallet. Let the
site ask to enable WebLN again. See [Moving a site to another account](#moving-a-site-to-another-account).
